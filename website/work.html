Work

The same round, five ways of looking at it — because the sheet you work from in the morning and the question "how big is my round?" are not the same thing.

Paper — the sheet you take out

The paper view is the day's work laid out like the sheet a round has always been worked from: grouped by round and by street, with a record column to mark each house off as you go. Mark a job done, skipped or cancelled right on the sheet, with the notes and tags beside each house.

List — the work in hand

The work list is what is due now and over the next fortnight — the work in hand, not the whole round for ever. From here you can:

  • Swipe a job done, skipped or over to its other actions.
  • Tap a tag on any row — the street, the town, the price, the money owed — to see everything else like it. While a filter is on, a bar above the list says exactly what you are looking at, with a Clear one tap away.
  • Pick jobs out — hold a row or use Select Jobs — to book a batch in, tag them, put them on a round or send them to someone. Tap a street's tag and then Select All, and you have picked the street.

All Jobs — everything you have

One row per house, for the whole round: what it pays, when it is next due, how often it comes round, and what the customer owes. Grouped by round, area, town and street, so a round of hundreds of houses still reads like a round and not like a spreadsheet. It answers the question the other pages cannot: what have I actually got?

Quotes — priced up, not taken on

A quote is work you have priced but not started, so it never shows up as work due or counts in the round's figures. The quote form asks only what pricing a job needs — where, what, how much, how often. Accept a quote and it becomes a real job, keeping its price and frequency; delete it and it is gone.

Stats — the round in figures

What is left to do, what is overdue, and what the round itself amounts to: how many houses, how long they all take, what one time round is worth and what it earns per month with everything done on time. A "By round" card breaks the same figures down one round at a time — and tapping a round opens All Jobs showing just that round's houses, so a figure that raises a question comes with the houses behind it.

Counted properly: what is left to do is counted in visits, the way the work list counts; how big the round is is counted in houses. Two visits due at one house is two jobs on the list, one house on the round — the figures never muddle the two.

Rounds

A round is a patch of the work — a village, a day of the week, however yours is really split. Put whole batches of work on a round from the work list rather than a house at a time, filter any page down to one round, and both work pages sort the rounds first with unorganised work last. The next clean at a house stays on the same round as the last one, automatically.

Tags and the tag bar

A tag says what this visit was like — front only, nobody in, gate locked. Tags show on the job rows and in the customer's history, so you can see which visits were like that. Because a day is usually all the same, the tag bar sets tags once for the whole day: everything you mark done gets them automatically, and un-marking a job takes them off again. Job types, tags and round names are all yours to edit on the settings page.
