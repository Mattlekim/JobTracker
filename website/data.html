Your data & backups

Your round is your livelihood. It lives in plain files on your phone, and getting it out — and back in — is deliberately boring.

It stays on your phone

Work Tracker needs no account and sends nothing anywhere. Customers, jobs, payments and expenses are files on your device, with everything the taxman cares about kept one file per tax year — so a finished year sits untouched with its receipts and statements filed beside it.

Backing up

A backup is one file holding whatever you choose: the whole lot, or just the tax years you pick — customers and jobs always ride along, because last year's figures are meaningless without the people they came from. Save it to your phone's downloads or share it anywhere a file can go: email it to yourself, drop it in Drive, hand it to your accountant. The tax page can also save any single year with its receipts and statements — a year's paperwork in one file.

Moving to a new phone

Install Work Tracker, then open your backup file from wherever it landed — an email, the downloads list, a file manager. The app offers to restore it, tells you plainly that restoring replaces what is on the device, and asks first. That is the whole move.

A backup you can open is a backup that works. Backups saved to the device are written so that tapping them offers Work Tracker back — and a file the app cannot read says so out loud rather than silently doing nothing.

Screenshot mode

Need to photograph the app — selling a round, asking for help, showing it off? Screenshot mode swaps every street, town and postcode on screen for made-up ones, consistently, so the app behaves exactly as it does on the real round while showing nobody's real address. It is display-only, never touches the data, and switches itself off on the next start so a round is never quietly running on made-up addresses.

Cloud sync — coming soon

Sync to your own Google Drive is built and waiting on its final credentials — the settings page says Coming Soon where it will live. It syncs to a private app folder on your Drive: still your data, still your account, just mirrored somewhere a lost phone cannot take it.
